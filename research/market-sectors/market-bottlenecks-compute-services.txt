definitions:
  hard technical limit: Constraint imposed by finite resources, physical distance, or a stated protocol/resource model. Software may improve efficiency or choose trade-offs, not remove the constraint.
  operational pain: Documented failure mode or delivery friction with existing technical approaches; prevalence and commercial demand require customer validation.
  market gap: Evidence of unmet integration, tooling, pricing, portability or workflow demand. A market gap does not establish willingness to pay or absence of competitors.
  speculative claim: A proposal or forecast without deployment evidence. Every Keddeh/BRAINK intervention in this register remains a hypothesis.
  distinct sector: A separately addressable failure mechanism, buyer outcome and acceptance test. Related sectors may share infrastructure, but retain distinct IDs, delivery owners and independent analyst teams.
normalization rules:
  - One bottleneck_id maps to one proposed sector_id, one delivery_team_id and a separate analyst_team_id.
  - Sector names are proposals, not assertions that teams, products or deployments already exist.
  - Problem metrics are measurements to collect; all numerical acceptance thresholds are proposed pilot gates, not published Keddeh results.
  - Source publication dates are exact only where verified. Undated living documentation is marked undated and carries an access date.
  - Foundational sources establish mechanisms, not 2026 prevalence. Surveys describe their respondent samples, not all enterprises.
  - No aggregate market-size or universal exhaustive-coverage claim is made.
  - Existing tools are genuine alternatives; Keddeh must outperform or usefully integrate them rather than rebrand them.
