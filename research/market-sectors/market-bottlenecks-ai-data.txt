definitions:
  Bottleneck: A scoped obstacle to a useful, safe or economical outcome, with an observable mechanism or evidence and a testable remediation hypothesis.
  Sector: A proposed market/problem accountability boundary; not an assertion that a canonical Keddeh registry sector exists.
  Foundry: An independently named Keddeh organizational/technical domain referenced by the parent; a relationship to multiple foundries does not merge them.
  Delivery team: Proposed BRAINK specialist team responsible for building or operating an authorized intervention.
  Analyst team: Separate proposed BRAINK team responsible for baseline, evidence, evaluation design, criticism and acceptance reporting.
  Reproduction: Same data and analysis rerun; computational repeatability alone does not establish truth.
  Robustness: Sensitivity of conclusions to reasonable changes in analysis using the same data.
  Replication: New-data test of the same scientific question.
  Primary evidence: Original study, official measurement, standard or first-party technical report; its type and limitations are retained.
  Service hypothesis: A proposed paid or internal intervention to validate, not an existing solved capability or guarantee.
controls and conventions:
  - Every sector identifier and BRAINK team charter in this register is proposed. Reconcile canonical registry identities without merging the independently named foundries.
  - Keep problem diagnosis, sourced capability, implementation, deployment, readback and independent verification as separate statuses. None of these entries is an implemented Keddeh service.
  - Each problem owns a delivery team and a separate analyst team. Shared specialists may advise, but analysts must not self-approve delivery or alter locked tests after seeing results.
  - All numerical acceptance gates are proposed local pilot criteria, not measured accomplishments, published industry standards or safety certifications. Adjust with the owner before the pilot and lock them before testing.
  - Report denominators, dates, task slices, confidence intervals, baseline quality, costs and negative outcomes. Zero observed failures never proves zero future risk.
  - Market demand, market size, willingness to pay and Keddeh qualification are unverified. Sources establish problem mechanisms or bounded measurements; proposed buyers and services are analyst inferences.
  - Do not make high-impact decisions autonomously. Require authorization for access, sensitive data use, production changes, red teaming, external communication and human-subject studies.
  - Prefer existing standards and maintained products when they satisfy the need. A Keddeh service must demonstrate a better measured outcome than those alternatives.
  - A byte hash establishes identity and custody, not scientific validity, truth, safety or qualified cryptography.
  - No private customer data was searched, published or transmitted for this annex. No teams, services, credentials or infrastructure were provisioned.
